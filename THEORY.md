# Theory development — editorial and maintenance notes

Added October 6, 2026 at the user's request. This is a selective historical synthesis of the site's circuits, not an exhaustive history of attention, a ranking of theories, or a claim of scientific priority. Ten shared histories contain 47 milestones and cite 65 unique library records. Ten foundational/theoretical/methodological records were added to the library, bringing it to 108. Older sources are not presented as new research.

## Structure and reading paths

`theoryHistories` in `region-content.js` contains the circuit narratives. Each milestone names its date, evidence type, finding/proposal, editorial interpretation of the change, and supporting references. Each history ends with a current synthesis and unresolved questions.

`regionTheory` gives all 17 nodes a specific interpretation and an ordered set of relevant histories. The first history appears in full within that region's Theory development tab; additional histories are linked. `connectionTheory` maps all 31 edges to surrounding circuit histories. The connection view retains the edge's existing strength, interpretation, caveat and references. A contextual history does not upgrade a hypothetical edge into demonstrated anatomy or causality.

The hub is `#theory`; a history is `#theory/control`; a region section is `#region/md/history`; a connection context is `#connection/md-pfc/history`. The header, overview, node panels, connection panels, region Orientation summaries and relevant digest cards provide entry points. Browser Back and keyboard navigation work across these routes. No animation is required to read the material.

## Historical and evidential decisions

- **Sensory gating:** Crick (1984) is an original hypothesis, not an experiment validating the complete searchlight mechanism. Retinal transmission physiology is distinct from voluntary attention. Mouse modality selection and macaque spatial attention need not yield the same LGN effect size. The PFC influence on sensory TRN is explicitly indirect via basal ganglia.
- **Competition:** Moran & Desimone (1985), the Desimone & Duncan (1995) framework, Reynolds et al. (1999), Roelfsema et al. (1998), and Reynolds & Heeger (2009) illustrate related, partially overlapping developments. The 1998 V1 result qualifies task-specific null effects, not an assertion that everyone previously believed V1 was unaffected. Normalization and the 2026 learning account address different explanatory levels.
- **Priority:** Covert inactivation tasks and subthreshold FEF stimulation are distinguished from recorded LIP priority and FEF–V4 coupling. Shared control of memory and attention does not establish identical coding in every region.
- **Pulvinar routing:** Guillery (1995) is an anatomical synthesis and proposal. Berman & Wurtz identify SC–pulvinar–MT, not the unresolved SC–dorsal-pulvinar priority arrow. Mouse LP subdivisions and primate pulvinar groups remain distinct. State signals to mouse PM and anesthetized macaque V1–pulvinar–V2 results constrain different preparations.
- **Pulvinar selection:** Human lesions, macaque reversible inactivation, confidence measures and signal-detection analyses address different ambiguities. Kaduk, Wilke & Kagan (2024), stored under the existing `kagan2024` key, refine the sensitivity-versus-criterion distinction in their task; this is not a universal null perceptual role for pulvinar.
- **Timing:** Brookshire (2022) is included as a substantive methodological challenge to some behavioral-rhythm analyses. It does not disprove all neural oscillations. Boshra et al. (2026) adds intervention evidence for bursts, without establishing a universal clock or a necessary endogenous theta-to-burst mechanism.
- **Control:** Fuster & Alexander (1971) already recorded both PFC and MD. Avoid a fictional history in which the thalamus appeared only recently. Miller & Cohen (2001) is an original integrative theory/review; population models and subsequent causal circuit studies address how control could be implemented. Lam et al. appeared online in 2024 and in a 2025 issue; the timeline states both, retaining the existing citation key/year.
- **Monitoring:** Conflict monitoring and expected value of control are attributed to their original theoretical papers. Mouse prediction-error interventions and tree-shrew transthalamic switching are not combined into one experimentally demonstrated pathway. These competing accounts are not declared settled by a single task.
- **Orienting:** Nigro-collicular output, caudate signals, SC inactivation and MD corollary discharge are kept distinct. Preserved MT/MST attentional enhancement during SC inactivation constrains the sufficiency of that measure, not all cortical contributions. Residual V1-lesion behavior does not localize a bypass route.
- **Engagement:** The 2007 single-patient study, 2020 macaque CL intervention, 2023 human feasibility study and 2026 centromedian ultrasound study have different targets and endpoints. Wake-like behavior, subjective consciousness, clinical benefit and spatial selection are not equated.

## Source review

New records were checked against primary publication records, primary manuscripts or the authors' original theoretical papers: Crick 1984 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC345636/)); Moran & Desimone 1985 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4023713/)); Reynolds & Heeger 2009 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2752446/)); Fuster & Alexander 1971 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4998337/)); Miller & Cohen 2001 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11283309/)); Botvinick et al. 2001 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11488380/)); Shenhav et al. 2013 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3767969/)); Wurtz & Goldberg 1971 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4992313/)); Schiff et al. 2007 ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06041)); Brookshire 2022 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35680992/)).

Existing library evidence was reused at its documented scope, with targeted primary-source rechecks of the main interpretive transitions: early gating, pulvinar coordination and subdivisions, choice versus sensitivity, SC versus cortical enhancement, thalamofrontal amplification/switching, ACC prediction errors, and CL stimulation. Some direct publisher requests returned browser challenges; indexed primary abstracts and accessible PMC manuscripts supplied the supported claims. No quantitative details were inferred from inaccessible methods. Recent-paper access limitations in RESEARCH.md still apply.

## Maintaining this section

Add a milestone when evidence changes or meaningfully constrains an interpretation; do not append every new paper. Explain whether the work supports, refines, challenges, or addresses a different question from earlier accounts. Preserve earlier proposals and substantive counterevidence. Cite an original proposal as theory and keep empirical studies separate. Keep species, tasks, subdivisions, publication dates and limits explicit.

After edits, run `node scripts/version-content.cjs` and the README's browser/data tests. The tests require region/edge coverage, resolvable history citations, source links, deep links, filter behavior, map entry points, browser Back, keyboard access, local-file loading and mobile overflow checks. Inspect changed desktop/mobile layouts before publication. This historical synthesis does not advance the weekly literature-search checkpoint.
